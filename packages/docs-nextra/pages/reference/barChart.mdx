# `<barChart>{:dn}`

<ComponentDisplay name='barChart'/>

`<barChart>{:dn}` draws one bar per number it is given. Pair it with
[`<tally>{:dn}`](tally) or [`<binCounts>{:dn}`](binCounts) to chart a sampling
simulation.

Bare numbers work as well as `<number>{:dn}` children, so `<barChart>41 63 18</barChart>`
draws three bars.

Every bar carries its category and value as an annotation, so a screen reader can walk the
chart rather than merely announce it, and a `<shortDescription>{:dn}` becomes the
description of the figure as a whole.

<AttrPropDisplay name='barChart'/>

## Examples

### Example: a chart of counts


```doenet-editor-horiz
<barChart categories="North South East West" displayValues>
  <shortDescription>Population by region</shortDescription>
  <yLabel>people</yLabel>
  <number>41</number>
  <number>63</number>
  <number>18</number>
  <number>78</number>
</barChart>
```



`displayValues` prints each bar's value above it.



---

### Example: charting a simulation


```doenet-editor-horiz
<setup>
  <numberList name="pop">30 45 12 60</numberList>
  <textList name="labels">North South East West</textList>
  <cumulativeSum name="cum">$pop</cumulativeSum>
  <number name="total"><sum>$pop</sum></number>
</setup>

<sampleRandomNumbers name="draws" type="discreteUniform"
  from="1" to="$total" numSamples="60" hide />
<searchSorted name="which" target="$draws" hide>$cum</searchSorted>
<tally name="counts" categories="1 2 3 4" hide>$which</tally>

<barChart categories="$labels">
  <shortDescription>Where 60 sampled individuals came from</shortDescription>
  <yLabel>count</yLabel>
  $counts
</barChart>

<callAction actionName="resample" target="$draws">
  <label>Draw again</label>
</callAction>
```



Five tags hold the whole simulation:
[`<sampleRandomNumbers>{:dn}`](sampleRandomNumbers) draws the sample,
[`<cumulativeSum>{:dn}`](cumulativeSum) accumulates the subpopulation sizes,
[`<searchSorted>{:dn}`](searchSorted) maps every draw onto the subpopulation it landed
in, [`<tally>{:dn}`](tally) counts them, and `<barChart>{:dn}` draws the counts.

Because `<tally>{:dn}` is given its categories rather than left to discover them, a
subpopulation that draws nothing still counts zero and keeps its bar, instead of dropping
out and shifting the others along.



---

### Example: a histogram of a continuous quantity


```doenet-editor-horiz
<setup>
  <numberList name="edges">-3 -2 -1 0 1 2 3</numberList>
  <textList name="bins">-2.5 -1.5 -0.5 0.5 1.5 2.5</textList>
</setup>

<sampleRandomNumbers name="z" type="gaussian" mean="0" variance="1"
  numSamples="200" hide />
<binCounts name="h" bins="$edges" hide>$z</binCounts>

<barChart categories="$bins">
  <shortDescription>200 draws from a standard normal distribution</shortDescription>
  <yLabel>count</yLabel>
  $h
</barChart>

<callAction actionName="resample" target="$z">
  <label>Draw again</label>
</callAction>
```



[`<binCounts>{:dn}`](binCounts) turns the sample into counts per interval and
`<barChart>{:dn}` draws them. Each bin is named by its midpoint rather than its range.



## Attribute Examples

### Attribute Example: categories


```doenet-editor-horiz
<barChart categories="North South East West" size="small">
  <shortDescription>Population by region, its bars named</shortDescription>
  <number>41</number><number>63</number><number>18</number><number>78</number>
</barChart>

<barChart size="small">
  <shortDescription>The same four values with no categories</shortDescription>
  <number>41</number><number>63</number><number>18</number><number>78</number>
</barChart>
```



Each bar takes the category in the matching position; without `categories`, the bars are
numbered 1, 2, 3, 4. A category is a label and nothing else — the bars are evenly spaced
whatever it says, so `categories="1 5 6"` writes 1, 5, 6 under three equally spaced bars
rather than placing them at those positions.



---

### Attribute Example: yMin, yMax


```doenet-editor-horiz
<barChart name="auto" categories="A B C" size="small">
  <shortDescription>An axis chosen from the data</shortDescription>
  <number>4</number><number>9</number><number>2</number>
</barChart>
<p>Automatic: $auto.yMin to $auto.yMax</p>

<barChart name="capped" categories="A B C" size="small" yMax="20">
  <shortDescription>The same bars under a fixed top</shortDescription>
  <number>4</number><number>9</number><number>2</number>
</barChart>
<p>With `yMax="20"`: $capped.yMin to $capped.yMax</p>

<barChart name="cut" categories="A B C" size="small" yMin="5">
  <shortDescription>An axis starting above zero</shortDescription>
  <number>4</number><number>9</number><number>2</number>
</barChart>
<p>With `yMin="5"`: $cut.yMin to $cut.yMax</p>
```



The axis scales itself to the data, and either bound may be replaced on its own. Bars are
measured from zero whatever the axis shows, so the third chart cuts them off at the
bottom of the frame and the bar of height 4 disappears entirely.



---

### Attribute Example: size, aspectRatio, barWidth


```doenet-editor-horiz
<barChart categories="A B C" size="small">
  <shortDescription>Default shape</shortDescription>
  <number>4</number><number>9</number><number>2</number>
</barChart>

<barChart categories="A B C" size="small" aspectRatio="1">
  <shortDescription>As tall as it is wide</shortDescription>
  <number>4</number><number>9</number><number>2</number>
</barChart>

<barChart categories="A B C" size="small" barWidth="0.4">
  <shortDescription>Narrow bars</shortDescription>
  <number>4</number><number>9</number><number>2</number>
</barChart>
```



`size` sets the width, `aspectRatio` the width over the height, and `barWidth` how much
of its slot each bar fills. A `width` may be given instead of a `size`, and picks the
nearest preset rather than being used exactly, so `width="400px"` draws the same chart as
`size="medium"`.



---

## Property Examples

### Property Example: barValues, categories, yMin, yMax


```doenet-editor-horiz
<barChart name="chart" categories="A B C" size="small">
  <shortDescription>Three values</shortDescription>
  <number>4</number>
  <number>9</number>
  <number>2</number>
</barChart>

<p>Values: $chart.barValues</p>
<p>Categories: $chart.categories</p>
<p>Total: <sum>$chart.barValues</sum></p>
<p>The axis runs from $chart.yMin to $chart.yMax.</p>
```



`yMin`, `yMax`, `barWidth` and `aspectRatio` report what the chart was drawn with rather
than what was asked for, so `$chart.yMax` is the top of the axis you can see even though
nothing above set one.
