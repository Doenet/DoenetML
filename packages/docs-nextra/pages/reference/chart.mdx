# `<chart>{:dn}`

<ComponentDisplay name='chart'/>

`<chart>{:dn}` draws data, and `type` picks which chart is drawn — there is no default, so
every chart names one. To chart several groups of data together, give the chart a
`<series>{:dn}` child per group.

<AttrPropDisplay name='chart'/>

## Chart types

Each type reads its data a little differently, and what a `<series>{:dn}` holds is what
changes. The title, the legend and the sizing are written the same way whichever chart is
drawn.

### `type="bar"`

One bar per value, standing on a baseline of zero, under a horizontal axis of category
names. A series holds one value per category.


```doenet-editor-horiz
<chart type="bar" categories="North South East West" displayValues size="small">
  <shortDescription>Population by region</shortDescription>
  <yLabel>people</yLabel>
  41 63 18 78
</chart>

<chart type="bar" categories="small medium large" size="small" legendPosition="upperRight">
  <shortDescription>Orders by size in two months</shortDescription>
  <yLabel>orders</yLabel>
  <series><label>March</label>8 15 6</series>
  <series><label>April</label>11 13 9</series>
</chart>
```





### `type="line"`

A path through each series' values, in the order given. A series holds one value per
category, or a value per `x` when it has one.


```doenet-editor-horiz
<chart type="line" categories="Mon Tue Wed Thu Fri" size="small">
  <shortDescription>Visitors per weekday</shortDescription>
  <yLabel>visitors</yLabel>
  12 19 15 22 30
</chart>

<chart type="line" size="small">
  <shortDescription>Position against time</shortDescription>
  <xLabel>seconds</xLabel>
  <yLabel>meters</yLabel>
  <series x="0 0.5 1 1.5 2">0 1.2 4.9 11 19.6</series>
</chart>
```



Without an `x` the points sit under `categories`, evenly spaced; with an `x` they sit at
the positions it gives and the axis carries numbers. Values are never re-ordered — a path that doubles back is drawn as one.



### `type="scatter"`

One point per value, drawn at the `x` beside it. A series holds the vertical coordinates
and its `x` the horizontal ones.


```doenet-editor-horiz
<chart type="scatter" size="small">
  <shortDescription>Weight against height for two groups</shortDescription>
  <xLabel>height</xLabel>
  <yLabel>weight</yLabel>
  <series x="1.5 1.6 1.7 1.8"><label>control</label>55 62 70 79</series>
  <series x="1.5 1.6 1.7 1.8"><label>treated</label>58 66 72 84</series>
</chart>
```



### `type="pie"`

One slice per value, each one that value's share of the total. The slices run clockwise from
twelve o'clock in the order the values are given, and `categories` names them. A pie draws
one series.


```doenet-editor-horiz
<chart type="pie" categories="North South East West" size="small">
  <shortDescription>Population by region</shortDescription>
  41 63 18 78
</chart>

<chart type="pie" categories="Wind Coal Solar" size="small" legend="false" displayValues>
  <shortDescription>Generation by source</shortDescription>
  40 35 25
</chart>
```



The slice names go in the legend, and around the rim instead when there is no legend to hold
them. `displayValues` writes each value out at the rim as well, beside its slice's name when
that is there too — as `Wind (40)` in the second chart. Each slice takes a color of its own,
where every other type colors by series.



### `type="box"`

A box plot per series, side by side. A series holds raw observations — a column of
measurements — rather than one value per category, and the box drawn from it runs from the
first quartile to the third with the median across it.


```doenet-editor-horiz
<chart type="box" size="small">
  <shortDescription>Scores in one section</shortDescription>
  <yLabel>score</yLabel>
  52 61 63 68 70 71 75 78 84 91
</chart>

<chart type="box" size="small">
  <shortDescription>Scores in two sections</shortDescription>
  <yLabel>score</yLabel>
  <series><label>9am</label>52 61 63 68 70 71 75 78 84 91</series>
  <series><label>1pm</label>44 55 58 60 62 65 66 70 72 96</series>
</chart>
```



Each series is one position on the axis, named under its box by its own `<label>{:dn}` — or
by its position, as the single box above is, where there is no label to name it. The
whiskers reach the furthest observation within one and a half interquartile ranges of the
box, and an observation past that is drawn as a point of its own — the 96 in the second
section. The vertical axis is the data's, so it need not include zero.

Each `<series>{:dn}` also reports its own summary — `minimum`, `quartile1`, `median`,
`quartile3`, `maximum` and `outliers` — computed the same way `<summaryStatistics>{:dn}`
computes them, so a table beside the picture agrees with it.



### `type="histogram"`

One bar per bin, adjacent with no gap, over a numeric axis of cut points. A series holds raw
observations, and each bar is as tall as the number of them that fall in its bin. A
histogram draws one series.


```doenet-editor-horiz
<chart type="histogram" size="small">
  <shortDescription>Heights of twenty seedlings</shortDescription>
  <xLabel>height (cm)</xLabel>
  <yLabel>how many</yLabel>
  4 5 5 6 6 6 7 7 7 7 8 8 8 9 9 10 11 12 12 13
</chart>

<chart type="histogram" size="small" bins="0 5 10 15">
  <shortDescription>The same heights in bands of five</shortDescription>
  <xLabel>height (cm)</xLabel>
  <yLabel>how many</yLabel>
  4 5 5 6 6 6 7 7 7 7 8 8 8 9 9 10 11 12 12 13
</chart>
```



Write no `bins` and the cut points are chosen from the data, at a width that keeps them
round numbers; write a number and the range is divided into that many equal bins; write two
or more numbers and they are the cut points themselves, as in the second chart above. The
horizontal axis is labeled at the cut points, and a value falling exactly on one is counted
in the bin above it unless `closed="right"` says otherwise.

The chart reports the bins it drew: `binEdges` is the cut points in order and `binCounts` is
how many fell in each, so a sentence, a table or an `<answer>{:dn}` can be written beside the
picture. [`<binCounts>{:dn}`](binCounts) counts the same data the same way, for the counts
without a picture.



## Examples

### Example: a bar chart of counts


```doenet-editor-horiz
<chart type="bar" categories="North South East West" displayValues>
  <shortDescription>Population by region</shortDescription>
  <yLabel>people</yLabel>
  41 63 18 78
</chart>
```



The values are written as plain numbers, separated by spaces. `<number>{:dn}` and
`<math>{:dn}` children work too, as does a reference to anything that produces numbers,
which is how a chart of computed values is written. `displayValues` prints each bar's
value above it.

Every bar carries its category and value as an annotation, so a screen reader can walk the
chart rather than merely announce it, and a `<shortDescription>{:dn}` becomes the
description of the figure as a whole.



---

### Example: several series in one chart


```doenet-editor-horiz
<chart type="bar" categories="North South East West">
  <title>Population by region</title>
  <shortDescription>Population by region, 2024 against 2025</shortDescription>
  <yLabel>people</yLabel>
  <series><label>2024</label>41 63 18 78</series>
  <series><label>2025</label>45 60 22 80</series>
</chart>
```



Each `<series>{:dn}` is one group of the data, drawn in its own color. Its children are the
values — bare numbers, `<number>{:dn}` and `<math>{:dn}` children, a
[`<numberList>{:dn}`](numberList), or a reference to something that produces numbers — and
its `<label>{:dn}` names it in the legend, which appears as soon as a series has one.
Series take consecutive style numbers unless one is given a `styleNumber` of its own, so
the groups come out in different colors without being asked to. A screen reader walks the
chart series by series, then mark by mark within a series.

A series may also carry an `x`, which gives its values horizontal coordinates and puts the
chart on a numeric axis instead of the categories. `type="scatter"{:dn}` is normally drawn
that way, and `type="line"{:dn}` may be; both fall back to the categories when no series
carries an `x`.

A chart written with bare values and no `<series>{:dn}` has one unnamed series holding them
all, which is why the simpler charts here need no series at all.

A `<title>{:dn}` is drawn above the chart and travels with the picture, so it is still
there if the chart is printed or exported.



---

### Example: a bar chart of a simulation


```doenet-editor-horiz
<setup>
  <numberList name="edges">-3 -2 -1 0 1 2 3</numberList>
  <textList name="bins">-2.5 -1.5 -0.5 0.5 1.5 2.5</textList>
  <sampleRandomNumbers name="z" type="gaussian" mean="0" variance="1"
    numSamples="200" />
  <binCounts name="h" bins="$edges">$z</binCounts>
</setup>

<chart type="bar" categories="$bins">
  <shortDescription>200 draws from a standard normal distribution</shortDescription>
  <yLabel>count</yLabel>
  $h
</chart>

<callAction actionName="resample" target="$z">
  <label>Draw again</label>
</callAction>
```



[`<binCounts>{:dn}`](binCounts) turns a sample into counts per interval and `<chart>{:dn}`
draws them; [`<tally>{:dn}`](tally) does the same for named categories. Building a whole
sampling simulation this way is walked through in
[Charting a Simulation](../guides/charting-a-simulation).

---

### Example: a box plot beside the numbers behind it


```doenet-editor-horiz
<setup>
  <numberList name="scores">52 61 63 68 70 71 75 78 84 91</numberList>
</setup>

<chart type="box" size="small">
  <shortDescription>Scores in one section</shortDescription>
  <yLabel>score</yLabel>
  <series name="section">$scores</series>
</chart>

<summaryStatistics statisticsToDisplay="fiveNumberSummary">$scores</summaryStatistics>

<p>The middle half of the section scored between $section.quartile1 and
$section.quartile3.</p>
```



A box plot and a [`<summaryStatistics>{:dn}`](summaryStatistics) of the same numbers report
the same five, so the table and the picture can stand side by side. Naming the
`<series>{:dn}` is what makes each of them readable into a sentence.

### Example: a histogram and the counts behind it


```doenet-editor-horiz
<setup>
  <numberList name="times">
    3.1 3.4 3.8 4.0 4.2 4.2 4.4 4.5 4.5 4.6
    4.7 4.9 5.1 5.2 5.4 5.8 6.1 6.4 7.2 7.9
  </numberList>
</setup>

<chart name="race" type="histogram" size="small" bins="3 4 5 6 7 8" displayValues>
  <shortDescription>Finishing times of twenty runners</shortDescription>
  <xLabel>minutes</xLabel>
  <yLabel>runners</yLabel>
  $times
</chart>

<p>The bins run from $race.binEdges[1] to $race.binEdges[6] minutes, and hold
$race.binCounts runners.</p>

<answer>
  <label>How many runners finished in under five minutes?</label>
  <number><sum>$race.binCounts[1] $race.binCounts[2]</sum></number>
</answer>
```



The counts are numbers the document can read, which is what lets the same bins be drawn,
described in a sentence and asked about without being written out three times.

## Attribute Examples

### Attribute Example: type


```doenet-editor-horiz
<chart type="bar" categories="A B C" size="small">
  <shortDescription>Three values as bars</shortDescription>
  4 9 2
</chart>

<chart type="line" categories="A B C" size="small">
  <shortDescription>The same three values as a line</shortDescription>
  4 9 2
</chart>

<chart categories="A B C" size="small">
  <shortDescription>The same values again, with no type</shortDescription>
  4 9 2
</chart>
```



`type` picks the mark the data is drawn with, not the data itself: all three charts above
hold the same three values. Two of them are the same numbers under different marks, which
is the whole of what this attribute does — everything else about a chart is written the
same way whichever type is named.

There is no default, so the third draws nothing and says so.

Every type is listed under [Chart types](#chart-types), with what a series holds for each.



---

### Attribute Example: categories


```doenet-editor-horiz
<chart type="bar" categories="North South East West" size="small">
  <shortDescription>Population by region, its bars named</shortDescription>
  41 63 18 78
</chart>

<chart type="bar" size="small">
  <shortDescription>The same four values with no categories</shortDescription>
  41 63 18 78
</chart>
```



Each bar takes the category in the matching position; without `categories`, the bars are
numbered 1, 2, 3, 4. A category is a label and nothing else — the bars are evenly spaced
whatever it says, so `categories="1 5 6"` writes 1, 5, 6 under three equally spaced bars
rather than placing them at those positions.

With several series, the categories run along the axis and the series are what the legend
names, so a chart of four categories and two series has four category names and two series
labels.

A bar, line or pie chart names its positions this way, and the two types whose series holds
raw observations do not: a box plot's positions are its series, named by their own labels,
and a histogram's are its bins, named by the cut points they run between.



---

### Attribute Example: bins, closed


```doenet-editor-horiz
<chart name="auto" type="histogram" size="small">
  <shortDescription>Bins chosen from the data</shortDescription>
  1 2 2 3 3 3 4 4 5 8
</chart>
<p>Chosen: $auto.binEdges</p>

<chart name="five" type="histogram" size="small" bins="5">
  <shortDescription>The same data in five equal bins</shortDescription>
  1 2 2 3 3 3 4 4 5 8
</chart>
<p>With <c>bins="5"</c>: $five.binEdges</p>

<chart name="cut" type="histogram" size="small" bins="0 3 6 9">
  <shortDescription>The same data at cut points of the author's</shortDescription>
  1 2 2 3 3 3 4 4 5 8
</chart>
<p>With <c>bins="0 3 6 9"</c>: $cut.binEdges, holding $cut.binCounts</p>
```



One number is a number of equal-width bins and is used exactly; two or more are the cut
points themselves, so `n + 1` of them make `n` bins. Written neither way, the bins are
chosen from the data: as many as the sample size suggests, at a width that keeps the cut
points round numbers.

`closed` says which end of a bin includes its cut point — `left` by default, so a bin runs
`[a, b)` and a value on a cut point is counted in the bin above it, and `closed="right"`
makes it `(a, b]`. The outermost cut point is included either way, so neither the smallest
nor the largest observation is left out. This is how
[`<binCounts>{:dn}`](binCounts) reads the same attribute, and both count the same data the
same way.

Only a histogram divides its data, so these are the histogram's alone.



---

### Attribute Example: layout


```doenet-editor-horiz
<chart type="bar" categories="A B C" size="small" displayValues>
  <shortDescription>Two series side by side</shortDescription>
  <series><label>first</label>4 9 2</series>
  <series><label>second</label>6 5 7</series>
</chart>

<chart type="bar" categories="A B C" size="small" layout="stacked" displayValues>
  <shortDescription>The same two series stacked</shortDescription>
  <series><label>first</label>4 9 2</series>
  <series><label>second</label>6 5 7</series>
</chart>
```



Side by side by default, so the bars can be compared both across categories and across
series. `layout="stacked"` puts them one above another instead, so each category's total is
what you read; negative values stack downward from the baseline rather than through the
positive ones. A chart of one series looks the same either way.

`displayValues` labels each series separately in both, so a stacked bar reads as its
parts: the second chart's middle bar is 9 and 5 rather than 14. Give a stacked chart
segments too thin to label and the numbers will run together, since each is drawn at the
top of its own segment.

`layout` is a bar chart's alone: it says how several series share one category's slot, and
no other type puts two series in the same place.



---

### Attribute Example: legend, legendPosition


```doenet-editor-horiz
<chart type="scatter" size="small">
  <shortDescription>Two groups of measurements, the legend beside the chart</shortDescription>
  <series x="1 2 3"><label>before</label>4 9 7</series>
  <series x="1 2 3"><label>after</label>6 11 10</series>
</chart>

<chart type="scatter" size="small" legendPosition="outsideBottom">
  <shortDescription>The same legend below the chart</shortDescription>
  <series x="1 2 3"><label>before</label>4 9 7</series>
  <series x="1 2 3"><label>after</label>6 11 10</series>
</chart>

<chart type="scatter" size="small" legendPosition="upperRight">
  <shortDescription>The same legend inside the plot</shortDescription>
  <series x="1 2 3"><label>before</label>4 9 7</series>
  <series x="1 2 3"><label>after</label>6 11 10</series>
</chart>
```



A legend appears as soon as a series has a `<label>{:dn}`, so a chart whose series are
unnamed never grows one — a legend names the series, and there is nothing to put in one
until they are named. `legend="false"` suppresses it even when they are.

`legendPosition` says where it goes. By default it sits **outside** the plot, to the right,
in a margin widened to hold it — so it never covers the data, at the cost of some of the
chart's width. `outsideBottom` puts it below the horizontal axis instead, spending height
rather than width, which suits long series labels or a wide chart.

The four inside corners — `upperRight`, `upperLeft`, `lowerRight`, `lowerLeft` — spend
neither, and are worth reaching for when the chart has a corner to spare. They can cover a
mark, as the third chart above does: room has to be made for one, either by a bound that
pushes the data clear of that corner or by choosing a corner the data does not reach.

On a pie the legend names the *slices* rather than the series, since a pie's colors tell its
slices apart. `legend="false"` there moves the names out to the rim, so they are
always somewhere:


```doenet-editor-horiz
<chart type="pie" categories="Renewables Coal Nuclear" size="small">
  <shortDescription>Generation by source, named in the legend</shortDescription>
  40 35 25
</chart>

<chart type="pie" categories="Renewables Coal Nuclear" size="small" legend="false">
  <shortDescription>The same three slices, named around the rim</shortDescription>
  40 35 25
</chart>
```



`$chart.showLegend{:dn}` reports whether a legend is drawn, so it is false for a suppressed
legend and for one with nothing to name — series that carry no label, or named series with
no value that could be drawn. On a pie there is something to name as soon as a slice is
drawn.

A bar, line, scatter or histogram chart's legend names its series, and a pie's names its
slices. A box plot draws none: its series are already named on the axis, under the box drawn
from each.



---

### Attribute Example: yMin, yMax


```doenet-editor-horiz
<chart name="auto" type="bar" categories="A B C" size="small">
  <shortDescription>An axis chosen from the data</shortDescription>
  4 9 2
</chart>
<p>Automatic: $auto.yMin to $auto.yMax</p>

<chart name="capped" type="bar" categories="A B C" size="small" yMax="20">
  <shortDescription>The same bars under a fixed top</shortDescription>
  4 9 2
</chart>
<p>With <c>yMax="20"</c>: $capped.yMin to $capped.yMax</p>

<chart name="cut" type="bar" categories="A B C" size="small" yMin="5">
  <shortDescription>An axis starting above zero</shortDescription>
  4 9 2
</chart>
<p>With <c>yMin="5"</c>: $cut.yMin to $cut.yMax</p>
```



The axis scales itself to the data, and either bound may be replaced on its own. Bars are
measured from zero whatever the axis shows, so the third chart cuts them off at the
bottom of the frame, and the two bars that end below 5 disappear entirely, leaving only
the one of height 9.

A bar chart's vertical axis always includes zero, because its bars are measured from it, and
so does a histogram's, whose bars are counts. A line, scatter or box chart's does not: its
marks are measured from nothing, so the axis scales to the data alone. A pie has no axes and
reads neither bound.



---

### Attribute Example: xMin, xMax


```doenet-editor-horiz
<chart name="auto" type="scatter" size="small">
  <shortDescription>An axis chosen from the data</shortDescription>
  <series x="1 2 3">4 9 2</series>
</chart>
<p>Automatic: $auto.xMin to $auto.xMax</p>

<chart name="wide" type="scatter" size="small" xMin="-5" xMax="15">
  <shortDescription>The same points on a wider axis</shortDescription>
  <series x="1 2 3">4 9 2</series>
</chart>
<p>With <c>xMin="-5" xMax="15"</c>: $wide.xMin to $wide.xMax</p>
```



The horizontal counterpart of `yMin` and `yMax`, and only for a chart whose horizontal axis
carries numbers — a histogram, and a line or scatter chart whose series carry an `x`. On a
categorical axis the positions are 1, 2, 3 whatever the categories say, so there is nothing
to bound: a bar chart, a box plot, and a line or scatter chart no series of which carries an
`x`, report nothing for them.



---

### Attribute Example: markers


```doenet-editor-horiz
<chart type="line" size="small">
  <shortDescription>A short series with its points marked</shortDescription>
  <series x="1 2 3 4 5">4 9 2 7 3</series>
</chart>

<chart type="line" size="small" markers="false">
  <shortDescription>The same series drawn as a line alone</shortDescription>
  <series x="1 2 3 4 5">4 9 2 7 3</series>
</chart>
```



A marker is drawn at each of a line chart's points unless `markers="false"` says otherwise,
which suits a series long enough for the markers to merge into the line. A scatter plot is
its markers, so it always draws them and ignores this.

The markers are also what a screen reader walks: each one carries its own annotation, and
with them off the line carries a single annotation for the whole series instead.



---

### Attribute Example: size, aspectRatio, barWidth


```doenet-editor-horiz
<chart type="bar" categories="A B C" size="small">
  <shortDescription>Default shape</shortDescription>
  4 9 2
</chart>

<chart type="bar" categories="A B C" size="small" aspectRatio="1">
  <shortDescription>As tall as it is wide</shortDescription>
  4 9 2
</chart>

<chart type="bar" categories="A B C" size="small" barWidth="0.4">
  <shortDescription>Narrow bars</shortDescription>
  4 9 2
</chart>
```



`size` sets the width and `aspectRatio` the width over the height, whichever chart is
drawn. `barWidth` is bar-only: it says how much of its slot each category fills, and the
series of a category divide that between them. A histogram's bars are adjacent, with no gap
to widen into, and a box's width is fixed. A `width` may be given instead of a `size`,
and picks the nearest preset rather than being used exactly, so `width="400px"` draws the
same chart as `size="medium"`.

## Property Examples

### Property Example: values, categories, numSeries, yMin, yMax


```doenet-editor-horiz
<chart name="counts" type="bar" categories="A B C" size="small">
  <shortDescription>Three values</shortDescription>
  4 9 2
</chart>

<p>Values: $counts.values</p>
<p>Categories: $counts.categories</p>
<p>Series: $counts.numSeries</p>
<p>Total: <sum>$counts.values</sum></p>
<p>The axis runs from $counts.yMin to $counts.yMax.</p>
```



`values` is every value in the chart, series by series, so a chart of one series reports
exactly what it was given; naming a `<series>{:dn}` is how one group is read on its own.

`yMin`, `yMax`, `xMin`, `xMax`, `barWidth` and `aspectRatio` report what the chart was
drawn with rather than what was asked for, so `$counts.yMax` is the top of the axis you can
see even though nothing above set one. `xMin` and `xMax` report nothing for a chart whose
horizontal axis carries category names rather than numbers, and a pie reports nothing for
any of the four: it has no axes to read a value off.

### Property Example: a series' own summary


```doenet-editor-horiz
<chart type="box" size="small">
  <shortDescription>Two sections' scores</shortDescription>
  <yLabel>score</yLabel>
  <series name="morning"><label>9am</label>52 61 63 68 70 71 75 78 84 91</series>
  <series name="afternoon"><label>1pm</label>44 55 58 60 62 65 66 70 72 96</series>
</chart>

<p>Morning median: $morning.median, afternoon median: $afternoon.median.</p>
<p>The afternoon section's middle half ran from $afternoon.quartile1 to
$afternoon.quartile3.</p>
<p>Drawn apart from the rest: $afternoon.outliers</p>
```



Every `<series>{:dn}` reports `minimum`, `quartile1`, `median`, `quartile3`, `maximum` and
`outliers` about the values it holds, whichever chart was drawn from them, so a sentence
beside the picture can say what the picture shows. `outliers` is the observations a box plot
draws as points beyond its whiskers.

### Property Example: binEdges, binCounts


```doenet-editor-horiz
<setup>
  <numberList name="scores">
    52 61 63 68 70 71 75 78 84 91 55 58 60 62 65 66 70 72 88 94
  </numberList>
</setup>

<chart name="h" type="histogram" size="small">
  <shortDescription>Twenty scores</shortDescription>
  <xLabel>score</xLabel>
  <yLabel>how many</yLabel>
  $scores
</chart>

<p>Cut points: $h.binEdges</p>
<p>Counts: $h.binCounts</p>
<p>The tallest bin holds <number><max>$h.binCounts</max></number> of the
<number><count>$scores</count></number> scores.</p>
```



A histogram reports the bins it drew, whether the cut points were written out or chosen from
the data, so a document can state them without repeating them. `binEdges` has one more entry
than `binCounts`: `n + 1` cut points make `n` bins.
