# Charting a Simulation

This guide is a set of recipes for turning a random sample into a chart a reader can watch
change: counting draws into categories, binning them into intervals, and comparing two
samples side by side. It assumes you already know how to write a `<chart>{:dn}` — see the
[`<chart>{:dn}` reference](../reference/chart) for its attributes and properties.

<Callout type="info">
Every recipe below counts the sample in DoenetML rather than inside the chart, so the counts
stay readable: `$counts{:dn}` can go into a sentence, a table, or an `<answer>{:dn}`
beside the picture.
</Callout>

---

## Count draws into named categories

[`<tally>{:dn}`](../reference/tally) counts how many values fall into each category, and
`<chart>{:dn}` draws the counts. The pieces are: draw the sample, map each draw onto a
category, count, chart.

```doenet-editor-horiz
<setup>
  <numberList name="pop">30 45 12 60</numberList>
  <textList name="labels">North South East West</textList>
  <cumulativeSum name="cum">$pop</cumulativeSum>
  <number name="total"><sum>$pop</sum></number>
  <sampleRandomNumbers name="draws" type="discreteUniform"
    from="1" to="$total" numSamples="60" />
  <searchSorted name="which" target="$draws">$cum</searchSorted>
  <tally name="counts" categories="1 2 3 4">$which</tally>
</setup>

<chart type="bar" categories="$labels">
  <shortDescription>Where 60 sampled individuals came from</shortDescription>
  <yLabel>count</yLabel>
  $counts
</chart>

<callAction actionName="resample" target="$draws">
  <label>Draw again</label>
</callAction>
```

Five tags hold the whole simulation:
[`<sampleRandomNumbers>{:dn}`](../reference/sampleRandomNumbers) draws the sample,
[`<cumulativeSum>{:dn}`](../reference/cumulativeSum) accumulates the subpopulation sizes,
[`<searchSorted>{:dn}`](../reference/searchSorted) maps every draw onto the subpopulation it
landed in, [`<tally>{:dn}`](../reference/tally) counts them, and `<chart>{:dn}` draws the
counts.

Give `<tally>{:dn}` its categories rather than leaving it to discover them. A subpopulation
that draws nothing then still counts zero and keeps its bar, instead of dropping out and
shifting the others along — which would make the chart jump every time the reader resamples.

`<callAction>{:dn}` redraws the sample, and everything downstream follows.

## Bin a continuous sample into intervals

For a measurement rather than a category, [`<binCounts>{:dn}`](../reference/binCounts) counts
how many values fall between each pair of cut points.

```doenet-editor-horiz
<setup>
  <numberList name="edges">-3 -2 -1 0 1 2 3</numberList>
  <textList name="bins">-2.5 -1.5 -0.5 0.5 1.5 2.5</textList>
  <sampleRandomNumbers name="z" type="gaussian" mean="0" variance="1"
    numSamples="200" />
  <binCounts name="h" bins="$edges">$z</binCounts>
</setup>

<chart type="bar" categories="$bins">
  <shortDescription>200 draws from a standard normal distribution</shortDescription>
  <yLabel>count</yLabel>
  $h
</chart>

<callAction actionName="resample" target="$z">
  <label>Draw again</label>
</callAction>
```

The cut points and the category names are two different lists: `bins` says where the
intervals begin and end, and `categories` says what to write under each bar. Naming each bin
by its midpoint, as here, keeps the axis readable; naming it by its range works too.

## Compare two samples in one chart

Give the chart a `<series>{:dn}` per sample. Each is counted separately and drawn in its own
color, and its `<label>{:dn}` names it in the legend.

```doenet-editor-horiz
<setup>
  <numberList name="edges">-3 -2 -1 0 1 2 3</numberList>
  <textList name="bins">-2.5 -1.5 -0.5 0.5 1.5 2.5</textList>
  <sampleRandomNumbers name="a" type="gaussian" mean="0" variance="1"
    numSamples="200" />
  <sampleRandomNumbers name="b" type="gaussian" mean="1" variance="1"
    numSamples="200" />
  <binCounts name="ha" bins="$edges">$a</binCounts>
  <binCounts name="hb" bins="$edges">$b</binCounts>
</setup>

<chart type="bar" categories="$bins">
  <title>Two samples, means 0 and 1</title>
  <shortDescription>200 draws from each of two normal distributions</shortDescription>
  <yLabel>count</yLabel>
  <series><label>mean 0</label>$ha</series>
  <series><label>mean 1</label>$hb</series>
</chart>

<callAction actionName="resample" target="$a">
  <label>Draw the first again</label>
</callAction>
<callAction actionName="resample" target="$b">
  <label>Draw the second again</label>
</callAction>
```

Both series are binned against the same cut points, which is what makes the bars comparable:
a bar in one series stands next to the bar for the same interval in the other.

Use `layout="stacked"{:dn}` instead when the two samples make up a whole and the total is
what a reader should read.

## Ask about what the chart shows

Because the counts are components, the same numbers that drew the chart can be asked about.

```doenet-editor-horiz
<setup>
  <numberList name="edges">-3 -2 -1 0 1 2 3</numberList>
  <sampleRandomNumbers name="z" type="gaussian" mean="0" variance="1"
    numSamples="200" />
  <binCounts name="h" bins="$edges">$z</binCounts>
</setup>

<chart name="c" type="bar" categories="-2.5 -1.5 -0.5 0.5 1.5 2.5" size="small"
  displayValues>
  <shortDescription>200 draws from a standard normal distribution</shortDescription>
  <yLabel>count</yLabel>
  $h
</chart>

<answer>
  <label>How many of the 200 draws landed between -1 and 1?</label>
  <number><sum>$h[3] $h[4]</sum></number>
</answer>

<p>The tallest bar holds <number><max>$c.values</max></number> draws.</p>
```

`$c.values{:dn}` is every value the chart drew, so a summary can be written beside the
picture and stay correct when the reader resamples.
