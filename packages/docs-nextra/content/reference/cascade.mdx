# `<cascade>{:dn}`

<ComponentDisplay name='cascade'/>

`<cascade>{:dn}` reveals its children one at a time as the learner makes
progress. Each child is hidden until the preceding child has been *completed* —
answered correctly (`creditAchieved = 1`), or having no `creditAchieved` at all
(in which case it is treated as auto-completed the moment it becomes visible).

The `numCompleted` property tracks how many children have been completed so
far. Use [`<cascadeMessage>{:dn}`](cascadeMessage) inside a `<cascade>{:dn}`
to show a "continuation message" in place of a not-yet-revealed entry.

A `<cascade>{:dn}` is not a section: controlling that step-by-step reveal is
all it does. It has no title, no number and no box of its own, and it does not
change the heading level or the numbering of the sections inside it — a
section inside a cascade is numbered and drawn exactly like the sections beside
it. A cascade has no score of its own either (no `weight`, no
`sectionWideCheckWork`), and it does not choose variants. For any of those, put
the `<cascade>{:dn}` inside a [`<section>{:dn}`](section) and give the section
the title, the box, the scoring or the variants, as in the examples below. A
`<title>{:dn}` written directly inside a `<cascade>{:dn}` is not shown, and a
warning suggests moving it to an enclosing section.

A section around a cascade is scored on the steps inside it directly, the same
as if the cascade were not there.

A `<cascade>{:dn}` automatically turns on `aggregateScores` on every scored
child (sections, `<problem>{:dn}`, nested `<cascade>{:dn}`s, even bare
`<p>{:dn}` paragraphs that contain answers). That is what gives each child a
`creditAchieved` value the cascade can watch — without it, a paragraph or
section containing an `<answer>{:dn}` would not roll the answer's credit up
into its own, and the cascade would have no way to tell when the child is
"done". You therefore do not need to set `aggregateScores` by hand on
cascade children.

A `handGraded` [`<answer>{:dn}`](answer2a#example-handgraded) is the one
exception to "answered correctly". Its credit stays at 0 until an instructor
grades it, which happens long after the learner has moved on, so a cascade would
otherwise stop there for good. Instead, such an answer counts as completed as
soon as the learner submits a response that is not blank — submitting an
untouched input does not count, except for a `<booleanInput>{:dn}`, whose
unchecked box is itself an answer. The answer's actual `creditAchieved` is
unchanged; it is still awaiting a grade.

The same rule colors the heading bar of a `boxed` or `collapsible` section, so a
step whose questions have all been answered shows as completed rather than
staying gray until an instructor gets to it. Set
[`completedColorRequiresCredit`](section#attribute-example-completedcolorrequirescredit)
on a section, on any section that encloses it, or on the cascade itself, to
have the bar wait for the real credit instead; that affects only the color,
never when the cascade advances. The cascade's `completedColor`,
`inProgressColor` and `notStartedColor` set the colors of its steps' heading
bars; when it sets none, its steps use the colors of the section around it.

Inside a [`<problems>{:dn}`](problems), the problems in a cascade are numbered
as items of the list, just as if they were written in the `<problems>{:dn}`
directly.

## Examples

### Example: Step-by-step questions


```doenet-editor-horiz
<section>
  <title>A short walkthrough</title>

  <cascade name="walkthrough">
    <section boxed>
      <title>First step</title>
      <p><answer><label>What is <m>1 + 1</m>?</label>2</answer></p>
    </section>

    <section boxed>
      <title>Second step</title>
      <p><answer><label>What is <m>3 \times 4</m>?</label>12</answer></p>
    </section>

    <section boxed>
      <title>Third step</title>
      <p><answer><label>What is <m>3 - 4</m>?</label>-1</answer></p>
    </section>
  </cascade>
</section>

<p>Sections completed: $walkthrough.numCompleted</p>
```


Each `<section>{:dn}` is revealed only after the previous one is answered
correctly. The `numCompleted` property of the named `<cascade>{:dn}` updates
as the learner progresses. The title belongs to the enclosing
`<section>{:dn}`, since the cascade has none of its own, and the three steps
are subsections of it.



---

### Example: Nested cascade inside a section


```doenet-editor-horiz
<section>
  <title>A walkthrough with a nested cascade</title>

  <cascade name="outer">
    <section boxed>
      <title>Warm-up</title>
      <p><answer><label>What is <m>2 + 2</m>?</label>4</answer></p>
    </section>

    <section boxed>
      <title>Two-step part</title>
      <cascade name="inner">
        <p><answer><label>What is <m>3 \times 4</m>?</label>12</answer></p>
        <p><answer><label>What is <m>12 \div 3</m>?</label>4</answer></p>
      </cascade>
    </section>

    <section boxed>
      <title>Cool-down</title>
      <p><answer><label>What is <m>10 - 7</m>?</label>3</answer></p>
    </section>
  </cascade>
</section>
```


The outer `<cascade>{:dn}` reveals its three `<section>{:dn}` children one at
a time. The middle section contains an inner `<cascade>{:dn}` whose children
are bare `<p>{:dn}` paragraphs. Because the outer cascade has turned on
`aggregateScores` for the middle section, and that section's inner cascade
turns it on for each `<p>{:dn}`, each paragraph rolls up the credit of its
`<answer>{:dn}` — so the inner cascade can hide the second paragraph until
the first answer is correct, then reveal it.



---

### Example: Cascade with continuation messages


```doenet-editor-horiz
<section>
  <title>A guided quiz</title>

  <cascade name="quiz">
    <section boxed>
      <title>Step 1</title>
      <p><answer><label>What is <m>2^3</m>?</label>8</answer></p>
    </section>

    <cascadeMessage>
      <em>Solve the previous step to continue.</em>
    </cascadeMessage>

    <section boxed>
      <title>Step 2</title>
      <p><answer><label>What is <m>3^2</m>?</label>9</answer></p>
    </section>

    <cascadeMessage>
      <em>One more step to go.</em>
    </cascadeMessage>

    <section boxed>
      <title>Step 3</title>
      <p><answer><label>What is <m>4^2</m>?</label>16</answer></p>
    </section>
  </cascade>
</section>
```


A [`<cascadeMessage>{:dn}`](cascadeMessage) replaces the entry that would
otherwise be hidden. One shows at a time — the first one after the last
revealed section — so here "Solve the previous step to continue" shows until
Step 1 is completed, at which point Step 2 appears and "One more step to go"
takes its place.



---

### Example: A scored, boxed cascade


```doenet-editor-horiz
<section boxed aggregateScores name="checkpoint">
  <title>Checkpoint</title>

  <cascade>
    <p><answer><label>What is <m>6 \times 7</m>?</label>42</answer></p>
    <p><answer><label>What is <m>42 \div 6</m>?</label>7</answer></p>
  </cascade>
</section>

<p>Checkpoint credit: $checkpoint.creditAchieved</p>
```


A cascade has no box or score of its own, so the enclosing
`<section>{:dn}` supplies both. Its credit comes from the answers inside the
cascade, the same as if they were written in the section directly.



## Attribute Examples

### Attribute Example: hideFutureSections


```doenet-editor-horiz
<section>
  <title>Sequenced steps</title>

  <cascade name="walkthrough" hideFutureSections>
    <section boxed>
      <title>Step 1</title>
      <p><answer><label>What is <m>5 + 5</m>?</label>10</answer></p>
    </section>

    <section boxed>
      <title>Step 2</title>
      <p><answer><label>What is <m>5 \times 5</m>?</label>25</answer></p>
    </section>
  </cascade>
</section>
```


With `hideFutureSections`, later children are completely hidden (titles and
all) until the preceding child is completed. Without it, the title of the
next section still appears greyed-out so the learner can see what is coming.



---

### Attribute Example: revealAll


```doenet-editor-horiz
<section>
  <title>All steps visible</title>

  <cascade name="walkthrough" revealAll>
    <section boxed>
      <title>Step 1</title>
      <p><answer><label>What is <m>5 + 5</m>?</label>10</answer></p>
    </section>

    <section boxed>
      <title>Step 2</title>
      <p><answer><label>What is <m>5 \times 5</m>?</label>25</answer></p>
    </section>
  </cascade>
</section>
```


Setting `revealAll` shows every child immediately, ignoring completion. This
is useful when authoring (so you can see every step) or when you want the
cascade *structure* without enforcing the order.



---

### Attribute Example: boxAll


```doenet-editor-horiz
<section>
  <title>All steps boxed</title>

  <cascade name="walkthrough" boxAll>
    <section>
      <title>Step 1</title>
      <p><answer><label>What is <m>5 + 5</m>?</label>10</answer></p>
    </section>

    <section>
      <title>Step 2</title>
      <p><answer><label>What is <m>5 \times 5</m>?</label>25</answer></p>
    </section>
  </cascade>
</section>
```


`boxAll` draws a box around every child section, as if each had been given
`boxed`. The cascade itself is never boxed; to box the whole cascade, put it in
a `boxed` section.



## Property Examples

### Property Example: numCompleted


```doenet-editor-horiz
<cascade name="w">
  <section boxed>
    <p><answer><label>What is <m>1 + 1</m>?</label>2</answer></p>
  </section>
  <section boxed>
    <p><answer><label>What is <m>2 + 2</m>?</label>4</answer></p>
  </section>
  <section boxed>
    <p><answer><label>What is <m>3 + 3</m>?</label>6</answer></p>
  </section>
</cascade>

<p>Completed: $w.numCompleted of 3</p>
```


The `numCompleted` property counts how many children have been completed so
far, in order. Once a child is skipped (e.g. its answer is wrong), counting
stops at that point.
